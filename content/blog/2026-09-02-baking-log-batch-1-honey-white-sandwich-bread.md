## 01. introduction

I've been baking our standard white sandwich bread for a little under a year now. The baseline recipe ([Tastes Better from Scratch](https://tastesbetterfromscratch.com/bread-recipe/)) yields two loaves, which works out to roughly 28 slices total, which is just about enough to cover two weeks of sandwiches before the next bake day rolls around.

Starting a small log here whenever a new batch comes out of the oven.

### The Specs
- **Recipe:** [Homemade Bread Recipe - Tastes Better from Scratch](https://tastesbetterfromscratch.com/bread-recipe/)
- **Flour:** King Arthur Unbleached Bread Flour
- **Salt:** 365 Coarse Sea Salt
- **Tweaks:** Subbed honey 1:1 for the granulated sugar

### The Bake & Result: 8/10

I could tell during the second rise in the pans that this batch was behaving nicely. The rise was uniform, domes looked okay, and didn't collapse or turn sluggish.

![Second rise in loaf pans](/media/second_rise.jpg)

Pulled them at the standard bake time

![Fresh out of the oven](/media/fresh_out_of_the_oven.jpg)
and immediately brushed the crusts with butter while they were still in the tins.

![Buttered crusts cooling in pans](/media/buttered.jpg)
Crumb shot on the cutting board once fully cooled:

![Half loaf sliced on cutting board showing crumb](/media/sliced.jpg)
Slices came out light and airy without feeling hollow.

Subjectively, my batches seem to turn out noticeably better when I swap in honey over sugar. It’s hard to isolate variables with bread—ambient humidity, proof temp, or slight hydration shifts could easily be the culprit (*eye roll* at baking consistency)—so who knows. That’s probably half the fun of doing it.

Next bake I'll flip back to granulated sugar just to run the A/B comparison again.
