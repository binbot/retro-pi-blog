Baking Log: Batch #5 - White Sandwich Bread

tags:#blog #bread-log #sugar

01. introduction

[fig: Hudson ~cat~ Cream]

Continuing the bi-weekly bread experiments using the Tastes Better From Scratch homemade bread recipe as the baseline. For Batch #5, I stuck with 100% Hudson Cream Flour and used granulated sugar again rather than honey.

Batch #5 Specs:

  • Recipe: Tastes Better From Scratch - Homemade Bread
  • Flour: 100% Hudson Cream (6 cups total)
  • Sweetener: Sugar
  • Salt: 365 Coarse Sea Salt

Something I haven't talked about in previous logs is the mixing aspect of bread-making. The recipe calls for 4 ½ to 5 ½ cups of flour, and I have never been able to use that little and still get to the point where the dough is no longer super sticky. For this bread, you want a dough where you can press your finger on it and not come away with dough stuck to your finger.

I've discovered that the amount of flour you end up using is heavily dependent on the mixing job at each step. It is important to fully mix in the flour each time you add it. That may seem obvious, but it is a time-consuming method that requires some patience to get right. When I first started making this recipe, it was often up to 7 cups of flour, and lately it has been 6 ½ cups. After making many batches and experimenting, this batch is the first where I've only used 6 cups of flour.

As an aside, you definitely don't want to use too much flour because the bread will turn out thick and heavy, not light and fluffy like sandwich bread should be. Getting down to only 6 cups of flour on this batch made me excited to see the difference it makes in the rest of the process.

Another variable at play is the temperature in the kitchen. The second half of October gets cooler around here, which combined with the sugar to slow things down. The first rise took longer than the usual two hours. I gave it another half hour and still wasn't sure about it. The second rise was the same, finishing at a bit lower height than I expected.

[fig: eh, it rose]

Despite the slower rise, it looked pretty decent fresh out of the oven.

[fig: footo]

I went to work buttering them up and noticed the sides had a nice texture and good color.

[fig: gloriously glazed]

Indeed, the texture is nice and the taste is great. Perfect for sandwiches.

[fig: Crumb shot on the board]

This batch gets a solid 8.5/10.
