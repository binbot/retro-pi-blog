Baking Log: Batch #1 (Honey White Sandwich Bread)

tags:#blog #bread-log #homemade #honey

01. introduction

I've been baking our standard white sandwich bread for a little under a year now. The baseline recipe (Tastes Better from Scratch) yields two loaves, which works out to roughly 28 slices total, which is just about enough to cover two weeks of sandwiches before the next bake day rolls around.

Starting a small log here whenever a new batch comes out of the oven.

The Specs

  • Recipe: Homemade Bread Recipe - Tastes Better from Scratch
  • Flour: King Arthur Unbleached Bread Flour
  • Salt: 365 Coarse Sea Salt
  • Tweaks: Subbed honey 1:1 for the granulated sugar

The Bake & Result: 8/10

I could tell during the second rise in the pans that this batch was behaving itself nicely. The rise was uniform, domes looked okay, and didn't collapse or turn sluggish.

[fig: Second rise in loaf pans]

Pulled them at the standard bake time

[fig: Fresh out of the oven]

and immediately brushed the crusts with butter while they were still in the tins.

[fig: Buttered crusts cooling in pans]

Crumb shot on the cutting board once fully cooled:

[fig: Half loaf sliced on cutting board showing crumb]

Slices came out light and airy without feeling hollow.

Subjectively, my batches seem to turn out noticeably better when I swap in honey over sugar. It’s hard to isolate variables with bread—ambient humidity, proof temp, or slight hydration shifts could easily be the culprit (eye roll at baking consistency)—so who knows. That’s probably half the fun of doing it.

Next bake I'll flip back to granulated sugar just to run the A/B comparison again.
